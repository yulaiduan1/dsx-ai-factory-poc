Power Source Scenarios

The DSX reference design supports three distinct power-source operating modes. Each one trades off cost, capital, time-to-revenue, and reliability differently. The figures here are from the Jacobs RAM Study (D4075100_RAM01, May 2026) — the same model NVIDIA uses to certify availability of any DSX deployment.

Availability targets

Two distinct reliability tiers — Core network loads carry a much higher target than the GPU compute building.

Core Network Loads

99.999% · Five 9's

~5.26 min downtime per year. The CORE block runs the control plane, storage, and management networks — everything that must outlast the GPU halls.

Compute Data Halls (GPU)

99.9% · Three 9's

~8.76 hr downtime per year. GPU training tolerates brief outages — restart from checkpoint is cheaper than building five-9's power for 240 MW.

The three scenarios

Each block shows the source topology, key availability results from the RAM analysis, and whether the design hits its target.

Scenario 01

100% Utility

3 feeds in N+1 · breaker-and-a-half

All site load is served directly from the grid. Three 230 kV utility feeds terminate at a common HV switching station configured breaker-and-a-half. Backup gens stand by only for Core; BESS handles transitions.

100% On-Premises Gas

4 gas blocks · N+1 · 4M3

Site runs entirely on natural-gas generation — four Bridging Power blocks in N+1 (4-to-make-3), each feeding a dedicated 34.5 kV MVS. Gas arrives from a high-pressure pipeline via a utility metering station.

What the RAM Study tells us

  • The MVS layer is the bottleneck. Up to MVS, every scenario clears five 9's. The gap to five 9's at the load comes from Common Cause Failures downstream of the MVS loops — primarily MV→LV transformers.
  • Automatic switching on Core MVS loops is essential. Manual switching adds hours of downtime per event. For GPU loads, a manual switch on the normally-open tie is acceptable.
  • Transformer MTTR drives CCF demand. If the 34.5 kV / 400 V transformer can be repaired in <168 hours (one week) instead of 720 hours (one month), the CCF control requirement relaxes dramatically.
  • Scenario 3 fails 3-9's at GPU unless the utility feed itself can guarantee >99.30% per feed and CCF between N+1 feeds is tightly controlled.
  • For Core five-9's: consider N+1 UPS per switchboard, redundant 230 kV substations or transmission lines, and physical segregation of redundant cables.

Detailed findings by load type

From Jacobs RAM Study §5.2 (base case) and §6.1 (conclusions).

Scenario GPU Loads (3-9's target) Core Loads (5-9's target)
1. 100% utility (N+1) Just above 3-9's at base utility (99%). With perfect utility, comfortable margin over 3-9's. Achieves 4-9's at the block level — misses 5-9's. CCF control downstream of MVS is the limiting factor.
2. 100% on-premises gas Achieves 3-9's. Higher gas-supply availability barely moves the needle. Achieves 4-9's. Same CCF-controlled gap to 5-9's. Auto switching on MVS still essential.
3. 50/50 hybrid Below 3-9's at base. Requires utility >99.30% per feed + tight CCF on N+1 feeds. Achieves 4-9's. Auto MVS switching essential; XFMR MTTR drives CCF tolerance.
